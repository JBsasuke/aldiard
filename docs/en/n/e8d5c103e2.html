Delta Technical Glitch Delays Thousands of Flights at Atlanta Airport

A technical failure at Delta has forced thousands of flights to be delayed at the world's busiest airport.

Key points

  • The failure is in Delta's weight and balance automation system.
  • As of Sunday noon, 48% of Delta flights were delayed, according to FlightAware.
  • The FAA says the delays are not related to its air traffic control systems.
  • The problem comes just over a week after an FAA outage halted departures across the United States.

Delta Air Lines reported a technical problem on Sunday that forced thousands of flights to be delayed at Atlanta's Hartsfield-Jackson International Airport, the busiest airport in the world. The Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) announced the incident.

According to the FAA, Delta is working to resolve problems with its weight and balance automation system. The agency said the delays are not related to any FAA air traffic system. Delta said in a statement that its teams are using manual solutions so flights can depart safely while the automation is restored. The airline did not disclose the cause of the failure.

According to the flight-tracking site FlightAware, as of Sunday noon, 48% of Delta flights were delayed. Some 38% of departures from Hartsfield-Jackson, Delta's operations hub, were also delayed, as were about 36% of arrivals to the airport.

The incident comes just over a week after an outage in the FAA's telecommunications network forced a suspension of departures across the United States for more than four hours on November 13. According to the FAA, about 12,462 flights were canceled that day, though not all were directly related to the national halt.

Delta is the only airline affected in this case. Another carrier, United Airlines, reported a "widespread systems outage" last week, adding to doubts about the reliability of the U.S. Aviation sector's technology infrastructure.

Why it matters

If you fly through or to Atlanta, your flight could be delayed. The situation also renews concerns about the reliability of the U.S. aviation sector's technology infrastructure.
