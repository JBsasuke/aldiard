Marilyn Monroe's Handwritten Recipe Sells for $16,000 at Auction

A chicken stuffing recipe in Marilyn Monroe's own handwriting sold for $16,000. A pencil note on the page has sparked speculation about her love life.

Key points

  • The recipe, dated to around 1954, sold for $16,000 at Julien's Auctions on June 4, 2026.
  • Its initial estimate was between $2,000 and $3,000.
  • Monroe wrote "Sin ajo" ("no garlic") in pencil at the top of the page.
  • The piece was part of the 100 Years of Marilyn auction, marking the centenary of the actress's birth.

A handwritten recipe by Marilyn Monroe sold for $16,000 at an auction of the actress's personal belongings, held June 4, 2026, at Julien's Auctions. The initial estimate was between $2,000 and $3,000, but bidding went well beyond that range.

The document, dated to around 1954, contains instructions for a chicken or turkey stuffing for Thanksgiving. It was part of 100 Years of Marilyn, an auction held to honor the centenary of the Hollywood star's birth. The manuscript came from the collection of Lee Strasberg, Monroe's acting coach, who inherited her personal belongings, and had been discovered more than a decade earlier.

One of the most striking details appears at the top of the page, where Monroe wrote in pencil: "Sin ajo" ("no garlic"). The note has sparked speculation about a possible link to Joe DiMaggio, the former New York Yankees player and the actress's second husband. According to Julien's Auctions, some sources say DiMaggio did not like garlic, so it has been suggested the recipe may have been written during their marriage. Monroe and DiMaggio married in January 1954 and separated nine months later, though they remained friends until the actress's death in 1962.

However, there is no conclusive evidence that the recipe was meant for the ballplayer. Scott Fortner, a researcher of Monroe's life and a collector of her belongings, told Fox News Digital that the paper used does not allow experts to determine with certainty when the recipe was written.

The manuscript lists about 18 ingredients, including sourdough French bread, chicken giblets, onion, celery, herbs, parmesan, raisins, walnuts, chestnuts, peanuts and hard-boiled eggs. Monroe also instructed that the bird be closed with thread or skewers before roasting, and recommended potatoes, mushrooms and peas as sides. The recipe is written on letterhead from City Title Insurance Company, with pencil notes on both sides.

The auction also included photographs, documents and personal clothing. The highest-priced items were an evening gown, sold for $32,000, and Monroe's first film contract with 20th Century Fox, signed in 1946, which sold for $76,800.
