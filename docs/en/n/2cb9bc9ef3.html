U.S. Embassy Warns of Drugging and Robbery Against Tourists in the Dominican Republic

The U.S. Embassy warns its citizens about cases of travelers drugged, robbed, and sexually assaulted in the country. This is what it recommends.

Key points

  • The Embassy reports cases of travelers drugged, robbed, or sexually assaulted
  • Some attacks happen after contact through dating apps or at bars and nightclubs
  • Incidents have occurred in short-term rentals and in and around hotels and motels
  • In emergencies, call 911 or the Tourist Police (POLITUR)

The U.S. Embassy in Santo Domingo urged its citizens to take extra caution regarding crime trends and personal safety when traveling to the Dominican Republic. The diplomatic mission receives periodic reports of travelers, including Americans, who have been drugged and then robbed or sexually assaulted.

In some cases, attackers target victims through dating apps or at bars and nightclubs. Attacks have also taken place in private residences, such as short-term rental properties, and in or around hotels and motels.

The Embassy recommends not leaving food or drinks unattended and not accepting food or drink from new acquaintances. When meeting a stranger, the safest option is to meet in public places and avoid isolated locations such as homes or hotel rooms. Before admitting a new visitor, it suggests alerting the concierge and establishing what identification the visitor must provide.

People who meet someone for the first time should tell a friend or family member about the plan, the location, and the app used. According to the alert, victims in these cases often have their phones stolen, which erases evidence of their communication.

In emergencies, people can call 911 or contact the Tourist Police (POLITUR) directly at 809-200-3500, which has English-speaking staff. There is also a WhatsApp number, 809-754-3096, and a "POLITUR APP." In case of sexual assault, POLITUR can guide victims on medical care and on notifying the specialized prosecutor's office. The Embassy is located at Av. República de Colombia 57, Santo Domingo, phone +1-809-567-7775.

Why it matters

The alert is aimed at U.S. citizens visiting the country, but its recommendations also apply to people who meet visitors or host new people at home. Caution is advised when meeting new people.
