Milagros Ortiz Bosch Says 111 Public Hospitals Are Debt-Free

111 public hospitals in the Dominican Republic are now debt-free, according to the Office of Government Ethics and Integrity. Here are the details.

Key points

  • Hospitals in the Dominican Republic's Public Health Network are debt-free.
  • Milagros Ortiz Bosch credits the result to financial management focused on responsible use of resources.
  • Julio Landrón, executive director of the National Health Service (SNS), describes a self-sustaining, transparent public health network.

Milagros Ortiz Bosch, director general of Government Ethics and Integrity, reported that 111 hospitals in the Public Health Network are currently debt-free. According to the official, the result stems from changes in how these health facilities manage their economic resources.

Ortiz Bosch made the announcement at an event led by President Luis Abinader, where she also praised the work of Julio Landrón, executive director of the National Health Service (SNS). In her view, the financial clean-up of the hospitals is a step forward in organizing and overseeing the resources allocated to medical care.

Ortiz Bosch recalled that the General Health Law and the legislation creating the Dominican Social Security System provide for economic autonomy and financial management mechanisms for hospitals. She considered that having 111 centers with no outstanding obligations shows an improvement in the health system's administrative processes.

"Today, 111 public hospitals have no debt, they are up to date. There is a new way of seeing health administration, and the Dominican people will quickly see a different way of enjoying health," the official said.

For his part, Landrón said the institution is working to strengthen resource management and comply with public procurement rules. "We are moving toward a self-sustaining Public Health Network that complies with all public procurement processes and regulations and whose management is characterized by transparency," he stated.

Authorities say tighter spending controls can make budget planning and the continuity of medical services easier. However, no details were given during the event about the amount of debt settled, the period in which it was cleared, or how many hospitales still have outstanding obligations.

Why it matters

If you use a public hospital in the Dominican Republic, this result could be reflected in how the centers are managed, though officials have not detailed its concrete impact.
