MLB Proposes $1 Million Minimum Salary and New Player Bonuses

MLB sweetens its offer to the Major League Baseball Players Association with a record minimum salary increase and new bonuses for players with and without arbitration rights.

Key points

  • The minimum salary would rise from $780,000 in 2026 to $1 million in 2027, a $220,000 increase.
  • Pre-arbitration players with a full year of service would receive at least $1.1 million in total.
  • An automatic 5% bonus would go to arbitration-eligible players who settle without a hearing.
  • Each club could give one veteran a $500,000 signing bonus per season.

Major League Baseball (MLB) has made a new economic offer to the Major League Baseball Players Association that includes a historic jump in the minimum salary and new bonuses funded by the league. The offer is part of MLB's push to install a salary cap-and-floor system.

The announcement came on a Monday, one week before the current Collective Bargaining Agreement expires. MLB spokesman Glen Caplin rejected the union's claim that the plan would harm "all categories of players." According to Caplin, the proposal raises pay at every stage of a player's career, and he noted that compensation growth in baseball has lagged behind every other major U.S. League, all of which have salary caps.

The most notable change is the minimum salary. The offer would raise it from $780,000 in 2026 to $1 million for all players in 2027, a $220,000 increase that would be the largest single-season jump in baseball history, surpassing the previous record of $129,500. Pre-arbitration players who complete a full year of service and spend less than half the season on the injured list would see an even bigger raise: they would also receive at least $100,000 through the Pre-Arbitration Bonus Program, for a total of at least $1.1 million, or $320,000 more, a 41% increase over 2026.

The league said 57% of players on Opening Day 40-man rosters in 2026, about 715 in total, would have benefited from the new minimum. Starting in 2028, the first year of the proposed cap-and-floor system, the minimum would grow alongside both limits.

For players eligible for arbitration, MLB proposes an automatic 5% bonus for reaching a settlement without a hearing, funded by the central league and paid before Opening Day. Under the current agreement, 93% of these players already settle before a hearing. The bonus would apply to both one-year and multi-year contracts, provided they do not cover free-agent years. For example, a player signing a $4 million deal would receive an extra $200,000. The league estimates the program would distribute about $33 million a year to an average of 175 players.

The proposal also lets each club award a $500,000 bonus to one veteran player per season. To qualify, the player must have at least six years of Major League service and sign a one-year contract worth $10 million or less per year.

MLB added that it remains open to other mechanisms to reach an agreement.
