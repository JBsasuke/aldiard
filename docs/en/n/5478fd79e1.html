IMF Urges Dominican Republic to Target Subsidies and Reform Power Sector

The IMF wants the Dominican Republic to cut fuel and electricity subsidies. The 2026 fiscal deficit would rise to 4.2% of GDP.

Key points

  • The IMF calls for reviving power sector reforms and better targeting of subsidies.
  • The revised budget raises the 2026 fiscal deficit from 3.2% to 4.2% of GDP.
  • The deficit rises from RD$280,575 million to RD$365,968 million.
  • The IMF projects economic growth of 4.6% in 2026 and 4.4% in 2027.

The International Monetary Fund (IMF) recommended that the Dominican Republic prioritize reducing the cost of fuel subsidies and reviving reforms in the electricity sector. In its final statement, the agency said the goal is to safeguard fiscal sustainability and keep prices stable amid what it called "elevated global uncertainty."

The mission, led by Michael Perks, took place from November 11 to 20 as part of the 2026 Article IV consultation. Among the measures the agency proposes are better targeting of subsidies, gradual tariff adjustments, and reducing losses at electricity distributors.

The IMF noted that because the pass-through of higher global energy prices to domestic prices has been only partial, fuel and electricity subsidies have risen significantly. In June 2026, the government adopted a package of fiscal measures to contain the impact of higher fuel prices, but its full effect on revenues will only be seen by 2027.

Meanwhile, the government submitted to Congress a revision of the 2026 General National Budget that raises the fiscal deficit from 3.2% to 4.2% of gross domestic product (GDP). According to the proposal, the deficit rises from RD$280,575 million to RD$365,968 million. Because primary spending growth exceeds the limit set by the Fiscal Responsibility Law, authorities have proposed possibly activating the escape clause.

The agency also recommends developing a medium-term revenue strategy that includes rationalizing tax expenditures and strengthening tax administration. As for projections, the IMF estimates economic growth could reach 4.6% by the end of 2026 and 4.4% in 2027, before gradually converging toward 5% in the medium term. It does expect inflationary pressures to persist through 2027.

Why it matters

If the government cuts subsidies, fuel and electricity prices could change, affecting household budgets in the Dominican Republic.
