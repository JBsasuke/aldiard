Bukele Files to Run for Third Term in El Salvador

Salvadoran President Nayib Bukele formally registered his candidacy for a third consecutive term in the 2027 elections. His supporters rallied outside the electoral tribunal.

Key points

  • Bukele registered his candidacy with the Supreme Electoral Tribunal (TSE) on Thursday.
  • The general elections are scheduled for February 28, 2027.
  • A 2025 constitutional reform removed term limits and extended the presidential term to six years.
  • Bukele has between 2,000 and 2,500 soldiers in Haiti, depending on the source.

Salvadoran President Nayib Bukele registered his candidacy on Thursday with the Supreme Electoral Tribunal (TSE) for a third consecutive term in office. The general elections are set for February 28, 2027. Bukele arrived at the tribunal's headquarters in San Salvador accompanied by Vice President Félix Ulloa, who also filed his application for the post. Supporters waited outside holding signs that read "Bukele 2027."

According to CDN, Bukele urged his supporters to turn out en masse to vote next year so that "El Salvador does not return to the past." He also said that "El Salvador is now an example for many countries." Bukele, 45, enjoys strong popular support, partly because of his crackdown on gangs under a state of emergency. According to Telemundo, citing The Associated Press, his approval rating is around 90%.

The reelection became possible through a constitutional reform approved by the Legislative Assembly on July 31, 2025. According to CDN, the reform was passed in a single session without prior analysis or debate. It extends the presidential term from five to six years, eliminates the runoff, and removes limits on reelection. According to Telemundo, the elections, originally scheduled for 2029, were moved up to 2027.

Bukele has also taken a larger role abroad. According to CDN, Salvadoran troops are participating in Haiti after four soldiers disappeared on October 18 in Croix-des-Bouquets. The forces have helped retake sites including a police substation, the EDH power company headquarters, and the Sylvio Cator stadium. Bukele says this is not an invasion. On the size of the deployment, the sources differ: Telemundo, citing AP, reports 2,500 troops, and Univision says Bukele sent more than 2,000 soldiers.

Critics warn about the country's democratic direction. According to Telemundo, Human Rights Watch argues that Bukele has concentrated power by taking control of the courts and pressuring opponents and journalists. According to Univision, families of the disappeared also question official security figures.

Why it matters

A change in electoral rules in a country with close ties to the U.S. and a large migrant population can affect regional stability. Dominicans living in El Salvador or following regional politics should also take note.
