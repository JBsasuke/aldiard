Dominican Electoral Board and OAS Close Summit With Pact Against Disinformation

Electoral authorities from 25 countries agreed to strengthen public trust and fight disinformation, including content generated with artificial intelligence.

Key points

  • The XIX Inter-American Meeting of Electoral Authorities was held November 11–13 at Hotel El Embajador in Santo Domingo.
  • Delegations from 35 electoral bodies in 25 OAS member states took part.
  • Topics included disinformation, artificial intelligence, political financing, and the security of electoral technologies.
  • Colombia will host the next meeting in 2027.

The Dominican Central Electoral Board (JCE) and the Organization of American States (OAS) concluded the XIX Inter-American Meeting of Electoral Authorities on Friday. The event ran from November 11 to 13 at Hotel El Embajador in Santo Domingo and ended with a call to strengthen public trust, transparency, and the resilience of electoral institutions.

Delegations from 35 electoral bodies in 25 OAS member states attended, along with representatives of international observer organizations. The gathering had the support of the governments of Canada and Switzerland.

According to participants, electoral integrity does not depend only on voting day. It also depends on respect for democratic rules and on the conditions under which citizens exercise their rights. During the sessions, they warned about risks such as disinformation, polarization, corruption, social inequality, and pressures against the rule of law.

Topics discussed included electoral equity, political financing, the transparency and security of electoral technologies, strategic intelligence, and the fight against disinformation. The impact of artificial intelligence on the spread of manipulated content was also analyzed.

The closing session was led by JCE President Román Andrés Jáquez Liranzo. The OAS was represented by Gerardo de Icaza, director of the Department of Electoral Cooperation, and Cristóbal Fernández, head of its Electoral Cooperation Section, who recognized the JCE plenary for its support and the success of the meeting. Canadian Ambassador Brendan Sutton also attended.

Jáquez Liranzo thanked the OAS for hosting the meeting and said that sharing experiences helps anticipate risks and strengthen institutional capacity. He noted that, given the challenges posed by artificial intelligence and disinformation, these meetings help identify more effective responses.

The XX Inter-American Meeting of Electoral Authorities will be held in Colombia in 2027, organized by the National Civil Registry. Jáquez Liranzo expressed the hope that the next meeting will show concrete results drawn from the experiences shared in Santo Domingo.

Why it matters

Disinformation and manipulated content made with artificial intelligence can erode trust in the electoral processes that Dominicans follow.
